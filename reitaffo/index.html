REIT AFFO per share

A REIT (real estate investment trust) is a company that owns property, like stores, apartments or casinos, and collects rent from it. REITs must pay most of their profit out to shareholders as dividends, so investors care about how much cash the properties really generate.

Ordinary accounting profit doesn't capture that well. It subtracts depreciation, as if the buildings were wearing out and losing value every year, even though well-kept real estate often holds or gains value. It also swings with one-off events like selling a property.

AFFO (adjusted funds from operations) is the industry's attempt at a truer measure. Start with profit, add back depreciation, remove one-off gains and losses, then subtract the money actually spent keeping properties in good shape. What's left is roughly the cash available to pay dividends. AFFO per share divides that by the number of shares, so it's a rough guide to how much cash backs each share. If it grows steadily, the dividend has room to grow too.

Each company calculates AFFO a little differently, so it's best for comparing a company with its own past rather than for precise comparisons between companies. Every figure here comes from the company's own earnings releases, and each links to its source.
